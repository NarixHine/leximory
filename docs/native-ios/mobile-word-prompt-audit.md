# Mobile word prompt source review

Reviewed on 2026-10-19; NL shots revised and rechecked on 2026-10-20. The five active mobile guides contain 20 demonstration outputs. This record covers their lexical facts and presentation. It does not measure model hallucination rates.

## Presentation correction

The first mobile drafts changed the established annotation style and used elementary examples. The current guides preserve the language-specific presentation while teaching contextual distinctions in literary and expository passages.

- Japanese uses `**［词性］（读音／中文义）** 日文解释`, with an optional kanji spelling in the parentheses for kana selections. It does not append a separate invented sentence and Chinese translation. Four of its five shots adapt vocabulary and contexts from the existing article shots. The additional idiom demonstrates preserving a lexicalized negative and distinguishing understanding an argument from accepting it.
- Ruby is conditional on difficult or unfamiliar kanji. Both ruby and ordinary Japanese explanations appear in the shots.
- French retains the bold Chinese gloss, IPA, French explanation, and optional etymology/cognate fields. The shots now concern ratifying an entrenched imbalance, invoking a title for preferential treatment, and an apparently available freedom that cannot be exercised.
- NL now uses adult expository contexts in four languages. The shots distinguish rhetorical downplaying, an expert's reservations, security concerns used as pretexts, and reasons wielded as argumentative weapons. Following the English word guide, Chinese is limited to the concise bold gloss in the definition field; the definition, usage and invented example are in the language being learned, without a Chinese paraphrase or example translation. Etymology notes remain brief, with short glosses on related words.
- Classical Chinese retains the compact three-field dictionary style and contexts drawn from the existing article prompt. Its shots demonstrate an institutional counting unit, a historical diplomatic term, a contextual temporal sense, and an uncommon reading.

The Japanese prompt also distinguishes the particle を's pronunciation お from its spelling, supported by [Shogakukan](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%82%92-666108). It no longer uses an elementary particle as a demonstration shot.

Parser tests check field order, lemmas, optional fields, the Japanese header structure, conditional ruby examples, and selected contextual distinctions. For the four non-Chinese NL shots, they also reject Chinese text outside the bold definition gloss and require an italicized source-language example, following the English word guide. These mechanical checks do not establish linguistic truth or predict generated output quality. Constructed contexts are illustrative sentences, not attributed literary quotations.
